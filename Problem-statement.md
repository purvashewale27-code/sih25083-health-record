# Problem Statement: Digital Health Record Management System for Migrant Workers in Kerala

**Smart India Hackathon (SIH 2025)**  
**Problem Statement ID:** `SIH25083`  
**Category:** Software / Healthcare & Public Administration  
**Target Beneficiaries:** 3.5+ Million Interstate Migrant Workers across Kerala, State Health Department, Primary Health Centers (PHCs), Community Health Centers (CHCs), and District Hospitals.

---

## 1. Executive Background
Kerala employs over **3.5 million interstate migrant workers** (often referred to as *Guest Workers* / *Athidhi Thozhilalikal*), originating primarily from West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, and Uttar Pradesh. These workers form the backbone of the state's construction, manufacturing, agricultural, and hospitality sectors.

However, the transient and highly mobile nature of this workforce presents severe structural challenges to healthcare administration and surveillance in Kerala.

---

## 2. Key Healthcare Challenges & Bottlenecks

### A. Fragmented & Paper-Based Medical Records
- Migrant workers frequently migrate across districts (e.g., Ernakulam ➔ Palakkad ➔ Kozhikode ➔ Kannur) based on seasonal job availability.
- When seeking medical treatment, previous health history, prescriptions, allergy information, and diagnosis records remain untracked because medical records are maintained in isolated, paper-based registers or facility-specific systems.
- This results in redundant diagnostic testing, inappropriate prescriptions, and increased healthcare costs.

### B. Severe Language & Communication Barriers
- The majority of migrant workers communicate exclusively in regional native languages (Hindi, Bengali, Odia, Assamese, Bhojpuri), while local healthcare personnel and clinical documentation primarily use Malayalam and English.
- Language barriers lead to:
  - Misinterpreted symptoms during clinical consultations.
  - Poor comprehension of medication dosages and prescription instructions.
  - Inadequate identification of chronic conditions or life-threatening drug allergies.

### C. Occupational Health Vulnerabilities & Delayed Alerts
- Migrant workers operate in hazardous environments (dust, chemicals, construction sites, heavy industrial machines) with heightened exposure to:
  - **Occupational Respiratory Disorders:** Silicosis, Acute Bronchitis, Chemical Inhalation.
  - **Dermatological Conditions:** Occupational Contact Dermatitis, Chemical Skin Ulcers.
  - **Infectious & Endemic Diseases:** Dengue, Malaria, Tuberculosis, Waterborne Gastroenteritis.
- The lack of real-time clinical data aggregation prevents public health officers from detecting disease clusters or epidemic outbreaks early.

### D. Suboptimal Treatment Adherence & Follow-Up Tracking
- Due to lack of centralized contact logs and tracking mechanisms, follow-up consultations (e.g., TB DOTS courses, post-injury dressing, diabetes/hypertension monitoring) are frequently missed when a worker changes employers or camps.

---

## 3. Core Objectives & Requirements for the Solution

1. **Unique Portable Digital Health ID**:
   - Issue each migrant worker a standardized QR-code enabled Digital Health Identity (e.g. `KMH-2026-XXXXX`).
   - Enable rapid scanning at any healthcare checkpoint (PHC, CHC, Hospital, Mobile Camp) for instant record access.

2. **Unified Longitudinal Health History**:
   - Centralize patient records, clinical consultation encounters, diagnosis (ICD-10 coded), prescription regimens, vitals history, lab reports, and drug allergies into a single real-time database.

3. **Intelligent Automated Health Alert Engine**:
   - Automatically analyze patient vitals, diagnosis keywords, and clinical histories to trigger immediate high/medium severity alerts (e.g., severe hypertension, dengue symptoms, recurrent occupational dermatitis, critical drug allergies).

4. **Multi-District & Multi-Facility Access Control**:
   - Enable seamless record synchronization across all 14 Kerala districts and all facility tiers (PHCs, Mobile Medical Camps, Taluk & District Hospitals).

5. **Follow-Up & Adherence Monitoring**:
   - Automated flagging of pending and overdue follow-ups to ensure zero lost-to-follow-up patients across camps.
